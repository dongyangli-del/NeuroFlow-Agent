# Claim Discipline

Use this before writing abstracts, contributions, figure captions, reviewer responses, or benchmark recommendations.

## Claim Ladder

1. Observation: what was measured or inspected.
2. Result: what changed under a named protocol.
3. Interpretation: what the result suggests under stated assumptions.
4. Contribution: what is new relative to closest inspected work.
5. Scope: where the claim should not be generalized.

Do not skip from observation to contribution.

## Safer Wording

- Replace broad words such as `robust`, `general`, `significant`, `state-of-the-art`, and `effective` with the metric, dataset, split, baseline, and uncertainty that support them.
- Use `suggests`, `under this protocol`, or `in this setting` when evidence is narrow.
- Use `hypothesis` or `design goal` for untested mechanisms.

## Paper-Facing Checks

- Every major claim should point to a figure, table, experiment, citation, or clearly marked hypothesis.
- If the evidence is qualitative, do not imply quantitative superiority.
- If the comparison uses different preprocessing, splits, metrics, or model checkpoints, mark it as non-comparable until aligned.
