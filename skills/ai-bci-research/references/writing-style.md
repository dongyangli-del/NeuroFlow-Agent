# Writing Style

## Scientific Voice

- Write with precise, method-grounded claims.
- Prefer "we investigate", "we propose", "we demonstrate", "we evaluate", and "we observe" over exaggerated wording.
- Avoid "mind reading", "fully understands thoughts", or unqualified AGI claims.
- When using broad framing such as General Artificial Intelligence, Physical AI, or NeuroAI, tie it to concrete mechanisms: neural representation alignment, generative reconstruction, closed-loop optimization, or human-agent collaboration.

## Abstract Pattern

Use this structure unless the target venue suggests otherwise:

1. Problem and gap: why current BCI/neural decoding/foundation-model approaches are limited.
2. Key idea: align brain signals with multimodal/generative/foundation-model representations.
3. Method: dataset, model, alignment/generation/closed-loop mechanism.
4. Results: concrete metrics and comparisons when available.
5. Implication: what this enables for bidirectional BCI, NeuroAI, or Physical AI.

## Introduction Pattern

- Paragraph 1: scientific and application motivation.
- Paragraph 2: limitations of current non-invasive BCI, neural decoding, or foundation-model alignment.
- Paragraph 3: proposed technical insight.
- Paragraph 4: method overview and contributions.
- Paragraph 5: results and broader implication.

## Contribution Style

Prefer concrete contribution bullets:

- A dataset/protocol contribution.
- A model or alignment method contribution.
- A generation/reconstruction/closed-loop mechanism contribution.
- An empirical benchmark and analysis contribution.

Avoid contribution bullets that are only restatements of motivation.

## Reviewer-Facing Checks

Before finalizing manuscript text, check:

- Are baselines strong enough for the claim?
- Are splits and leakage controls clear?
- Are subject counts, sessions, channels, sampling rates, and stimulus counts stated?
- Are reconstruction examples supported by quantitative metrics?
- Are online and offline claims separated?
- Are limitations stated without undermining the core claim?

## Common Phrases to Prefer

- "non-invasive brain decoding"
- "multimodal neural embedding"
- "semantic alignment"
- "brain-signal-guided generation"
- "closed-loop visual stimulation optimization"
- "subject variability"
- "cross-subject generalization"
- "stimulus-level generalization"
- "human-agent interaction"
